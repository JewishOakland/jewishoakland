From the Rabbi  Draft for review
Shall Not the Judge of All the Earth Do Justice?

This week Abraham does something bold. Hearing that the cities of Sodom are about to be destroyed, he stands before God and argues. Will you sweep away the righteous with the wicked? Perhaps there are fifty righteous people in the city. Will you spare the place for their sake?

Notice that Abraham is not arguing for his own family. He is arguing for strangers, people he has never met, people who have shown no particular kindness to him. His whole life has been a training in caring for those outside his tent, and now he steps forward for them in the one place where a righteous person is least comfortable: in front of God, pleading.

Justice, in this portion, is not cold. It is the willingness to plead for people who cannot plead for themselves. Each of us has someone we would rather not speak up for, and each of us is asked, quietly, what we would argue for.

Shabbat gives us the time to do that. Come sit with us, and bring someone who might be a stranger to you today.
Rav Dovid Labkowski
