Friday, October 30, 2026 Parashat Vayera

From the Rabbi

The Tent Open on Every Side

Rav Dovid LabkowskiDraft for Rabbi's review

The Torah opens this week with Abraham sitting at the entrance of his tent in the heat of the day. He is recovering from his circumcision, and he has every reason to rest. Yet when he lifts his eyes and sees three strangers standing nearby, he runs to meet them, bows to the ground, and begs them to stay.

The Talmud tells us that Abraham's tent was open on all four sides, so that a guest could come in from any direction. Our tradition seems to have understood something important: hospitality is not a feature of a building. It is a way of standing.

Notice what Abraham does not do. He does not wait for the visitors to prove they are worthy. He does not ask who they are. He sees a need, and he acts. Only afterward does the story reveal who his guests were.

Each of us has a tent. For some it is a home; for others, a workplace, a classroom or a phone. The question Vayera asks is simple: which way does your entrance face?

Shabbat is the most open door in Jewish life. Whoever you are and however you arrived, you are welcome at our table this week. Come as you are.

Rav Dovid Labkowski
